AUREL

A beauty storefront designed more like a magazine edit than a fake marketplace: research a narrow drop, give the products room to breathe, then send the shopper to the real retailer.

Commerce rule

If we do not own checkout, we do not draw checkout.

AUREL links outward to the real retailer instead of simulating a cart, account system or payment flow. That keeps the current product small, truthful and ready for future affiliate tracking without redesigning the experience.

Drop10 researched products
ModelDiscovery + outbound retail
StackVanilla web

Editorial, not warehouse.

The design language uses warm neutrals, berry accents, oversized typography and restrained motion so the page reads as a curated opinion rather than an infinite product grid.

Research→Edit→Discover→Retailer

First drop, not final scope.

KIKO MILANO is the opening edit, not the permanent identity of the product. The architecture is intentionally multi-brand while avoiding any claim that AUREL is an official KIKO property.

Real imagery

Product presentation is based on official retailer assets instead of synthetic cosmetics pretending to be the item.

Affiliate-ready

Retailer URLs can later become tracked affiliate links without changing the shopper-facing interaction model.

Small stack

No backend, database, cart, accounts or payments are carried before the business model actually needs them.

The design problem

Make ten products feel like a point of view.

The interesting work is not displaying products. It is creating enough taste, hierarchy and pacing that the visitor understands why these ten belong together.
